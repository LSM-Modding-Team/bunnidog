# Intelligence update: player stories and acceptance scenarios

Planning only, 2026-10-21. These are proposed experiences, not completed tests or
implemented features. Read [INTELLIGENCE_PLAN.md](INTELLIGENCE_PLAN.md) for the
creative direction and [INTELLIGENCE_ENGINEERING.md](INTELLIGENCE_ENGINEERING.md)
for state, resources and delivery. “Demonstrate” below means actual native/world
play or an appropriate integration scenario; a matching string in source is not
proof. Natural pacing and emotional readability require client play.

## 1. The little companion that does not understand a delivery

I find an endearingly limited adult. It recognizes me, accepts food, finds its
home, wears a bundle and responds to an established return signal. When I ask it
to deliver supplies to the far cache, care says “Can carry a marked load with a
guide; cannot remember a delivery route.” It does not wander off, eat the cargo
or let me grind a training bar that can never finish.

I use it as a travelling pack animal, a familiar local helper and a parent. It
is still a good creature to keep, rather than a failed item to discard.

Demonstrate: a genuine low-capacity genotype loses the complex work available
today, while practical care, cargo retrieval and safety remain reliable. Its
reported limitation agrees with the actual eligibility check.

## 2. A first useful lesson without reading a book

My compatible adult watches me designate a carrot patch and an authorized store.
A short demonstration and a real successful load establish the routine. I see
it harvest an eligible crop, walk there and deposit the item. It can repeat a
small understood task without my commanding each step.

When the store fills, it waits nearby and says why. When I move the store, it
knows hauling but needs a new destination. It has learned a purpose, not a magic
permanent coordinate that it can access through walls and unloaded chunks.

Demonstrate: real items move exactly once; no invisible work stock exists;
changing the world invalidates a target without erasing the whole skill.

## 3. The surprising feral engineer

A fierce adult is mediocre at comforting a pupil but has strong spatial reasoning
and handling. It learns construction well. A gentle loyal animal is wonderful
company yet cannot plan a room. A skittish route specialist becomes a messenger
and an excellent danger observer.

I can breed or train any temperament toward useful work. I do not learn that
“loyal” secretly means smart, “feral” means incapable, or big ears determine IQ.

Demonstrate: cognitive requirements are separate from temperament/perception;
all temperaments can still form a familiar bond, and job restrictions are explained.

## 4. Two different minds make a better crew

One animal remembers long routes and communicates clearly. Another has exceptional
hands/paws but poor independent planning. Neither can establish a workshop alone.
Together, with a shared plan and the right materials, they are effective.

A less capable hauler joins by moving marked loads. Its supervisor sends a short
instruction rather than donating a hidden intelligence buff. If the supervisor
leaves, it finishes a safe understood step and waits for the next instruction.

Demonstrate: a supported task is simpler than independent planning, yet still
requires the executor's own relevant capacity, skill, tools and physical ability.

## 5. Breeding a kind of mind

At the Table I compare a route-minded coastal parent and a precise mountain one.
Their offspring have several possible profiles. I can see “can learn independent
building” separately from “can assist a builder.” Memory alone does not guarantee
it, and the coat/legs I want still affect the physical role.

A promising pup is born with a strong profile but no construction job. I raise
and teach it. A sibling with different genes becomes a useful courier instead.
Neither food nor ten thousand repetitions adds genetic points to these axes.

Demonstrate: the probability comes from both copies of several loci; real seed
and actual pup readouts agree; culture, job title and teacher memories are not
copied through the seed.

## 6. My old working family survives the update

I load a save with a trained party, a supply resident, a nursery line, occupied
baskets, named pedigrees, old samples, an infused bottle and an actual stored seed.
Their identity, health, injury, contents, parents, settings and familiar abilities
remain usable. Care explains their documented helper profile.

A new founder can be substantially more limited or more capable. Existing adults
can participate in the new world without all becoming architects for free. Old
samples and old live animals resolve to the same complete genotype.

Demonstrate: actual legacy fixtures through entity/item codecs; no gene truncation,
name loss, repeated migration lesson grants or failure of trusted recall.

## 7. The bright pup and the patient teacher

A gifted pup plays beside a practiced gardener. It watches real work and begins
a simple routine. It cannot use adult tools or make complicated decisions just
because its genes are strong. Later it studies a builder's plan with a different
mentor. Education remains local and tangible.

If I pause growth with a golden dandelion, the game respects that choice. If I
raise it quickly with Bloom and food, it can still learn as an adult. I do not
have to choose between useful Bloom and a permanently ruined education.

Demonstrate: personal learning is saved separately, age constraints apply, pupils
do not learn from an absent teacher, and maturity does not close an arbitrary window.

## 8. A farm with a real food problem

My farm has plenty of mushrooms, but two workers eat a different diet. The commons
reports suitable food shortages rather than a falsely healthy total of “80 food.”
A storekeeper reserves essential meals and breeding guavas. A grower changes a
suitable bed; a courier moves only surplus that actually exists.

A powered Trough pauses work even during a shortage. Residents look for another
safe authorized food source or rest; they do not bypass redstone or raid my chest.
Passive healthy pets elsewhere do not start starving because I built a town.

Demonstrate: Diet-aware stock, paid support, protected reserves and station policy
remain authoritative; shortage prompts meaningful work rather than invisible death.

## 9. The first room is genuinely built

A small community has more prospective residents than safe Nest places. A planner
surveys a valid small annex. I see the site marker and a short material list.
Carriers bring actual wood, a builder makes supports and walls, and a helper
places a known fitting under supervision. The entrance stays usable.

When finished, a resident uses its real Nest. I can walk through the room, count
the materials used and see that a useful physical thing changed. It did not
appear because a “village level” counter increased.

Demonstrate: staged paid world edits, native actor/protection checks, safe access
and actual occupancy. A tiny completed room is stronger evidence than twenty
unexecuted blueprints.

## 10. I put my own wall across their project

I interrupt a building site with a block or modify the doorway. The crew notices
“Site changed: doorway blocked.” It stops or offers a safe revision. It does not
dig my block every morning, clear a nearby container, or decide my base is a
convenient quarry. Ordinary visitors can build nearby without an escalating feud.

If I explicitly release that cell to the project, its authorized worker can use
it. The interaction is one useful plot decision, not an approval prompt per block.

Demonstrate: player edits are meaningful, negative permissions cannot be bypassed
by another worker, and retries/backoff do not create repeated destructive work.

## 11. The architect goes adventuring

I invite a town's planner to travel. A sufficiently recorded annex plan stays
available, and its capable builders finish the known stages. A helper with a
single-step instruction finishes that step and waits. No one invents a new
unsurveyed extension just because the original author once stood there.

Later the community trains a second planner. It becomes more resilient through
knowledge and people, not by electing a magic flag that supplies missing abilities.

Demonstrate: personal skill, accessible records and supported instructions have
separate lifetimes; essential behavior does not hinge on one online entity.

## 12. A family starts without me

I observe a newly seeded wilderness family at a suitable site. Without giving
orders, food, tools, metal or lessons, it gathers local food and material, makes
primitive stores/shelter, prepares a small suitable growing area and learns
repeatable work. It establishes real Nests before committing to a nursery.

After finite initial supplies run down, it still feeds its workers and can finish
a useful structure. Its success is slow enough to observe, with rests, exploration
and failed-but-explained attempts. A similar family lacking the needed combinations
can remain a stable simple colony instead.

Demonstrate: a complete resource/recipe bootstrap with no player/merchant dependency,
actual actor actions and no invisible civilization resource pool. This is a
mandatory end-to-end milestone, not a decorative worldgen demonstration.

## 13. Their first new generation

A supported town has room, caretakers and breeding supplies. Two suitable adults
use actual guavas. Their courtship creates the normal seed. A worker carries it
to a permitted nursery, grows it and hatches onto safe clear space. Real pups
learn from present residents.

If the nursery fills, births wait. If one parent has swarm fertility, the town
accounts for a larger litter rather than spawning endlessly. It does not breed
through a paused player station or spend a prized seed reserved in private storage.

Demonstrate: the existing seed/quality/parent flow, no spontaneous child spawn,
housing/food/population checks and actual teaching across a generation.

## 14. An archive is lost, but the town does not forget everything

An accident destroys a public record store. Practiced residents keep their own
skills. An unfinished specialist process may lose its only accessible procedure.
Another copy or a living mentor restores it. A curious capable worker can attempt
bounded rediscovery if the prerequisites are present.

A copied record is valuable cargo. It does not teach every pupil instantly or
work from inside an unloaded private chest. The town can decline or recover for
observable reasons, with no global knowledge switch erasing every individual.

Demonstrate: accessible provenance, durable personal skill, real record items,
bounded rediscovery and no invulnerable remote archive hidden in SavedData.

## 15. The community saves a friend

A valued worker is injured in ordinary combat. A trained carer and defenders can
make a safe rescue with real first-aid supplies and a real transport item where
the eventual civic rescue policy permits it. The rescued individual retains its
name, learning, possessions and recovery needs.

My bonded partners keep their existing downed/first-aid contract throughout the
update. Terrain hazards remain real; a smart society is not immune to lava or
the void. A complete lost crew can cost knowledge, so redundancy and public
records are useful.

Demonstrate: no automatic heal/reset, no endless paid-less rescue supplies, and a
clear distinction between already-shipped bonded rescue and a future citizen-care
extension. Do not call that civic extension implemented merely because pets recover.

## 16. A clever fighting partner still needs me

A limited bonded fighter understands a simple nearby Assist and return. A more
capable experienced one remembers a held position, distinguishes a reachable
interception and communicates a threat to the other chosen partners. Its body
still sets bite, movement and resilience.

Normal Whistle recalls every heard party member in either case. Advanced tactics
need observation and coordination; they do not attack an unseen opponent through
a wall or make an ordinary carrier join the fight. A town has local defenders,
not a free unbounded player army.

Demonstrate: genetic planning affects tactics above a dependable baseline, actual
hearing/terrain/legal targets still matter, and work intent releases promptly.

## 17. I visit a culture rather than a shopping machine

An island community uses coastal foods, remembers dry shore routes and makes a
small distinctive set of goods. A mountain workshop has different methods,
buildings and teachers. Either can become advanced through suitable individuals
and opportunities. Neither habitat owns the single mandatory genius allele.

A specialist I invite can share a learned technique at home. Its genome does not
copy that technique into offspring. The community offers repeat reasons to visit:
mentors, actual surplus, useful surveys, a public project and familiar residents.

Demonstrate: regional physical genes/resources matter without turning region into
an intelligence caste, and trade/education have value beyond harvesting founders.

## 18. Two towns exchange something that actually travels

Two self-supporting communities establish an agreement. One has an authorized
surplus and the other offers useful material or a record. A courier takes real
cargo along a surveyed safe route, rests and returns with the actual exchange.
A messenger can carry an offer before the material convoy exists.

If the intervening chunks unload, the journey pauses. A remembered destination
is not a teleportation endpoint. Disabling exports or losing a safe route makes
the task wait/return safely, not print an equal amount in both towns.

Demonstrate: no offscreen production/trade, bounded travel, leases and permission
revalidation, and exact item/record conservation across interruptions and reload.

## 19. A living ruin and a modest brilliant household

I find a grand old five-tree site with an intact hall but limited inhabitants.
They use it as a simple home. Elsewhere, a small household of complementary minds
has one tree, a working archive and a carefully maintained workshop.

Static tree count still describes the generated site's scale. Neither locality
is forced to copy the other's culture or grow to the same physical size. An old
settlement does not start tearing up a nearby established player base after an
update; live construction there requires deliberate activation/adoption.

Demonstrate: physical size, cognitive potential, cultural knowledge and civic
organization are genuinely separate states.

## 20. I keep playing my original way

I can still raise show lines, use a Juicer, keep cuddly pets, harvest living
materials, automate with hoppers, explore warrens and take a small combat party.
A useful teacher or worker can enrich those activities without making me found
a town. Existing body shows and physical commissions remain meaningful.

The new system supplies roles and reasons to breed, not an unavoidable management
game attached to every animal. I can leave a healthy passive family alone without
a universal daily feeding tax.

Demonstrate: the original first-litter and companion journeys still work, with
honest new limits and simple education where needed, without a civilization manual.

## 21. Changing genes during a job

I deliberately splice a capable worker's cognitive pair. It retains its identity,
health ratio and knowledge, but may no longer be able to execute the current job.
The job stops safely, returns/releases the right reservations and explains why.
A later suitable splice may make that learned procedure usable again.

Changing capacity does not generate output, reset injury, refill a tool, upload
new skills or let a worker keep spending from a job it can no longer perform.

Demonstrate: live capability re-evaluation and resource conservation across a real
existing genetic-edit path, not only a freshly spawned test animal.

## 22. Multiplayer stays trustworthy

Two players open the same worksite. One changes a donation policy, removes stock
or invites its worker away. The other's old control cannot spend the old stock,
claim the same worker or override the new permission. Both see a short useful
refresh reason. A third player's nearby storage stays private.

One player uses English and another uses Spanish. Names/agreements remain their
chosen text, while genetic roles, job reasons and town controls localize for each.
A citizen does not become owned by whichever screen opened last.

Demonstrate: per-recipient text, actual authority, revision/transaction checks and
clear residency/keeper/party separation.

## 23. The world waits while I am gone

I log out or move far away. Work in unloaded areas pauses, leases are reconciled,
and no hidden generations, taxes, disasters or exported goods accumulate. When
I return, the community resumes after checking actual stock, routes and people.
A partly loaded building cannot keep placing blocks beyond the loaded boundary.

Demonstrate: normal saves, individual unloads and reloads; no forced chunks or
wall-clock catch-up. Hard-crash reconciliation needs its own explicit evidence,
not a blanket promise based on one graceful server stop.

## 24. It is enjoyable to understand

In a fresh Survival session, I learn that one animal needs a demonstration,
another needs a teacher, and a public worksite needs planks by looking at them
and opening a short panel. Work props, patient gestures, actual material trips
and one blocking reason make the process legible.

I can use detailed Calipers/Table views when breeding seriously, but I do not
need to memorize four numbers to enjoy a learner or recognize a growing community.
Their approved animal bodies, little routines, affection and idle play remain.

Demonstrate: an unfamiliar player completes a simple lesson and understands a
stalled public project without the long guide or developer commands. Inspect
actual client behavior in both languages; headless tests cannot establish this.

## Review milestones

The first play review should combine 1, 2, 5 and 6: a limited but worthwhile animal,
a useful learner, inherited differences and a compatible old family. The first
construction review combines 4, 9, 10 and 11. The autonomy review must combine 8,
12, 13 and 14 with finite resources. The civilization review adds 15–19 and 22–24.
These groupings describe complete experiences, not extra check counts to inflate.
